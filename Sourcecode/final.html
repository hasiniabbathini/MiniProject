🟡 Understanding the LIME Map

What the highlighted regions mean

Highlighted regions — Most influential superpixels
LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) works by perturbing small regions of the image and observing how the model's prediction changes. The highlighted superpixels are the areas whose removal caused the greatest drop in the model's confidence. These regions directly influenced the classification decision. The size of the highlighted area is proportional to the tumor probability — a high tumor probability results in a larger highlighted zone, while a low probability shows only a small region.

🔬 Understanding the SHAP Map

What the colours mean for your image

Red regions — Driving the TUMOR prediction
Blue regions — Associated with NORMAL tissue
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) uses gradient-based saliency to compute how much each pixel contributed to the model's final decision. The brighter and more saturated the colour, the stronger that region's influence on the prediction. Regions with no overlay had little-to-no effect on the classification. This transparency allows clinicians to verify the model is looking at clinically relevant regions — not noise or image artefacts.
